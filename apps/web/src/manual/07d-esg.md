# ESG & materiality

Menu → Disclose → **ESG & materiality** covers the sustainability report beyond greenhouse gases. It works one reporting year at a time, in four tabs:

```flow
> Frameworks & set-up | choose frameworks → see which assessment they need → industry, thresholds, value chain, stakeholders
> Double materiality | score each topic for impact and financial effect → matrix → decide → second person approves
> ESG data | questions for the material topics and your industry, with the reference in each framework
> Content index | where each answer sits in each framework → Excel export
```

## Which framework needs which assessment

| Framework | Region | Assessment needed |
|---|---|---|
| ESRS (EU CSRD) | EU | **Double materiality** — required; only material topics are reported |
| GRI Standards | Global | **Impact materiality** (GRI 3) |
| IFRS S1 and S2 (ISSB) | Global | **Financial materiality** |
| SASB Standards | Global | Financial materiality, by industry |
| VSME (EU voluntary SME standard) | EU | None (basic module) |
| ADX ESG Disclosure Guidance | UAE | None — metrics-based; an assessment is recommended |
| DFM ESG Reporting Guide | UAE | None — metrics-based; recommended |
| Saudi Exchange ESG Disclosure Guidelines | Saudi Arabia | None — metrics-based; recommended |
| CDP | Global | None — questionnaire |

Tick the frameworks you report under. A banner tells you what they need together. For example, **GRI + IFRS S1/S2** needs both the impact side and the financial side, which in practice is a double materiality assessment, even without ESRS.

**Who is in ESRS scope** changes with EU law (the Omnibus I simplification raised the thresholds). Check the current rules with your adviser before relying on the description shown.

## Set-up

- **Industry** — one of 16 (waste management, utilities, real estate and construction, building materials, oil and gas, chemicals, mining, manufacturing, transport and logistics, hospitality, healthcare, food and agriculture, retail, technology, financial services, professional services). It decides:
  - which topics are marked **●** as usually material in your industry;
  - which industry-specific questions appear under ESG data.
- **Thresholds** — the score (1–5) at or above which a topic is material. The default is 3.0 on each side.
- **Value chain** — what upstream, own operations and downstream cover.
- **Method** — how the long list was built, who scored and how stakeholder input was used.
- **Stakeholders consulted** — group, how, when and how many people. ESRS and GRI expect affected stakeholders to be engaged.

## Double materiality

The long list is the ten ESRS topics: **E1** Climate change, **E2** Pollution, **E3** Water, **E4** Biodiversity, **E5** Resource use and circular economy, **S1** Own workforce, **S2** Workers in the value chain, **S3** Affected communities, **S4** Consumers and end-users, **G1** Business conduct. Open a topic with **Assess**.

**Impact (inside-out)** — the company's effect on people and the environment:

- Actual or potential, negative or positive, and where in the value chain.
- **Severity** = the average of *scale*, *scope* and *irremediability*, each 1–5. A positive impact uses scale and scope only.
- An **actual** impact scores its severity.
- A **potential** impact scores severity × likelihood ÷ 5.
- A **potential negative human-rights** impact scores its severity alone: severity takes precedence over likelihood.

**Financial (outside-in)** — the topic's effect on the company: risk or opportunity, time horizon, and **magnitude × likelihood ÷ 5**.

**Decision** — a topic is material when a score your frameworks need reaches its threshold. You can decide otherwise, but you must give the reason. The topic then shows "(decided)".

The **matrix** plots every assessed topic. Filled circles are material; the dashed lines are the thresholds.

**Suppliers' view** — suppliers who answer the optional *ESG impact* section of the supplier portal rate each topic 0–5. The table shows how many answered and their average; the topic drawer lists each answer. Use it as stakeholder input for upstream impacts.

**Approval:**

1. A super admin, admin or manager clicks **Send for approval** once every topic is assessed and at least one stakeholder group is listed.
2. A super admin or admin who did not send it or last change it approves it, or returns it with a note. Platform staff cannot approve.
3. Any later change returns an approved assessment to draft.

## ESG data

The questions shown are those of your **material topics** — or, before any topic is material, those usually material in your industry. There are two kinds:

- **General** questions for each topic, e.g. gross Scope 1, water withdrawn, injury rate, anti-corruption policy.
- **Industry** questions, e.g. tonnes of waste handled and landfill diversion for waste management, energy intensity of buildings for real estate.

Each question shows its reference in every framework you chose (ESRS, GRI, IFRS S2, ADX, Saudi Exchange, SASB). Answers are checked:

- numbers must be numbers;
- percentages are at most 100;
- yes/no questions take yes or no.

Greenhouse-gas questions (Scope 1, Scope 2 location- and market-based, Scope 3) are filled from your inventory. Type a value to overwrite it.

## Content index and export

**Content index** lists, for each framework, the reference, the disclosure, the value and whether it is answered, filled from the inventory or missing.

**Export (Excel)** gives a workbook with five sheets:

- the materiality results;
- the impacts, risks and opportunities with every score input;
- the ESG data with one reference column per framework;
- the content index;
- the method and stakeholders.

## Supplier portal: ESG impact

The supplier portal has an optional step, **ESG impact — double materiality**, with three parts:

- the supplier's industry;
- for each topic, a 0–5 rating of *their* impact and of the topic's effect on their business, with the main issue;
- a few facts: ISO 14001 and 45001, code of conduct, human-rights and anti-corruption policies, whistleblowing channel, share of women, injury rate, fatalities, renewable energy, water, waste, recycling, and a link to their report.

The step does not block submitting. You see the answers in the supplier's review drawer. They reach the double materiality page once the request is submitted or accepted. A supplier's answers are only ever seen by the company that asked.
